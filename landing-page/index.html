Georgia O'Keeffe Museum · Digital Experience

Ways into O'Keeffe's work

These prototypes turn Georgia O'Keeffe Museum data into things you can explore, from maps and timelines to works shown at their true size. Each is built in p5.js, a creative coding tool made for artists, with the help of generative AI. New experiments will be added here as they're ready. Try any of them, then tell us what you think.

Globe · 137 public collections

Find an O'Keeffe Near You

Turn a globe to find where you are, then click that spot to see the nearest public collections that hold her work. The 1,507 works on the globe are held in nine countries and territories.

True size · 2,024 works

O'Keeffe to Scale

View paintings, watercolors, pastels, and drawings in the catalogue raisonné, each drawn as an outline at its true size, beside a silhouette of O'Keeffe at 5 feet 5 inches. Play through her career year by year, or compare subjects side by side.

Bubble chart · subjects

O'Keeffe's Subjects

O'Keeffe's works grouped by what they show, from landscapes and flowers to bones, buildings, and abstractions. Click a bubble to break it apart into smaller subjects, down to the individual works. This is based on previous prototypes sketched with digital partners Design for Context.

Timeline · 1875–1986

O'Keeffe in Context

Moments from her life set beside world events and the art of her time, from 1875 to 1986. Select an event to read about it and see a work from the catalogue raisonné made the same year.

Quiz · five rounds

Eye for O'Keeffe

How well do you read an O'Keeffe? Look at two of her works and decide whether their compositions are similar. Then see how a computer reads them, and find out how often you agree over five rounds. This prototype is based on a computer vision prototype by Jonathan Lee from NMHU.

O'Keeffe Experiments is a digital experience lab at the Georgia O'Keeffe Museum. These prototypes use collection data, including the catalogue raisonné, that has not been reviewed for publication, along with some information added with the help of AI. Images are provided through the Museum's IIIF image service.
