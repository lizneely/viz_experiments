O'Keeffe in Tree Rings

Douglas-firs at Echo Amphitheater, a few miles from Ghost Ranch, add a ring of wood every year. Wide rings mark good growing years; narrow ones usually mark drought. Here are the 100 rings from 1887, the year Georgia O'Keeffe was born, to 1986, the year she died, with her works placed on the ring for the year they were made.

About the data

Two records that were never meant to meet: one kept by trees, one kept by a museum.

The trees

The rings come from Douglas-firs at Echo Amphitheater in the Carson National Forest, about four miles northwest of Ghost Ranch and 6,660 feet above sea level. C. A. Woodhouse, D. Griffin, M. V. Losleben, and V. Major sampled the site. Their record runs from 1295 to 2008 and is held in the International Tree-Ring Data Bank as site NM593.

The ring-width index averages 50 to 63 samples for each year of O'Keeffe's life and removes each tree's age trend, so 1.0 is an ordinary year. The cross-section is drawn from that average, not from a single tree. The oldest samples were already growing in 1295, nearly 600 years before O'Keeffe was born.

See the record at NOAA

Why a ring is a rain gauge

In dry country, these trees grow most in years with good winter and spring moisture, so a wide ring usually means a wet year and a narrow ring a dry one. Cold, late frost, fire, and insects can narrow a ring too, so read each ring as likely, not certain.

The works

The 1,265 works with images in Access O'Keeffe, the Georgia O'Keeffe Museum's digital catalogue raisonné; the same set as Curate Your Own Gallery. Of these, 725 are dated to a single year and 540 to a span, such as 1959–1960 or the 1930s. On the cross-section, works dated to a span are open circles placed somewhere within it. In the chart, each counts as a share of every year in its span: a work dated 1959–1960 adds half a work to each year.

How this was made

This page was made with the help of AI, which suggested pairing the collection with tree rings, found the nearest long ring record, matched it to the collection data, and drafted the code and text. The life events were written from general knowledge and have not been checked against the Museum's chronology.

The cross-section is drawn in p5.js. The ring shapes wobble for realism; only their width carries data.
