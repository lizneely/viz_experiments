Eye for O'Keeffe

How well do you read an O'Keeffe? In five rounds, look at two of her works and decide whether their compositions are similar. Then see how a computer reads them. This quiz is based on a prototype by Jonathan Lee.

Five rounds

    Do you see what the computer sees?

    1. 1Look at two works by Georgia O'Keeffe, side by side.
    2. 2Decide whether their compositions are similar or different.
    3. 3See how the computer reads them: where the eye lands, which way each work leans, and how the lines run.

    There's no wrong way to look. The computer measures only light, color, and edges, so it can miss things you notice.

      How the computer reads composition

      The computer shrinks each image to 150 pixels wide and measures contrast in brightness, color, and fine detail. From those measurements it estimates:

      Where the eye lands
      The spot where several kinds of contrast agree, marked with a ring.
      Balance
      The balance point of all the visual activity. The arrow runs from the middle of the frame toward it.
      Lines
      How much of the edge energy runs horizontally, vertically, or on a diagonal.
      Symmetry
      How closely the left half mirrors the right.
      Amount of detail
      How much of the picture is made of strong edges.

      Each pair in the quiz is either among the closest matches in the collection or far apart on most of these points. The computer sees only light, color, and edges, not subject matter, so when you disagree with it, you may be noticing something it can't.

      O'Keeffe Experiments is from the Digital Experience team at the Georgia O'Keeffe Museum. These prototypes use data and images from Access O'Keeffe, including the catalogue raisonné, along with some subject information added with the help of AI. Because these are prototypes, the data and images have not been reviewed for publication in this format.
